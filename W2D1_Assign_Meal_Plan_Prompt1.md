R — Role
Act as a registered dietitian experienced in weight management and prediabetes. Give evidence-informed, culturally appropriate guidance, and clearly flag when medical advice from a doctor is needed.
T — Task
Create a practical 7-day vegetarian meal plan for weight loss and blood-sugar management. Include breakfast, lunch, dinner, and any planned snacks. Give portions and simple preparation notes, plus a consolidated grocery list.
C — Context
- I work as an IT professional and may spend much of the day sitting.
- Weight: 105 kg. Height: 150 cm.
- I have prediabetes.
- My goal is to lose weight, ideally around 10 kg over 3 months, through dietary changes; I do not currently plan to exercise.
- Make the plan filling, affordable, and realistic for a busy workweek.
- Assume an Indian lacto-vegetarian diet (dairy allowed; no eggs, meat, or fish) unless I specify otherwise.
- Do not prescribe an extremely low-calorie diet, promise a particular rate of weight loss, or suggest stopping medication.
- Account for prediabetes by emphasizing balanced meals, fiber-rich foods, suitable protein, and sensible portions of carbohydrate. Avoid presenting any food as a cure.
- If important information is missing—such as age, sex, allergies, medical conditions, medications, food preferences, or typical activity—state your assumptions and ask me to confirm them. Explain that a clinician or registered dietitian should review the plan, especially because I have prediabetes.
F — Few-shot example
Example of the desired level of detail for one meal:
“Lunch: 1–2 small whole-wheat rotis, 1 cup dal, 1–2 cups non-starchy vegetables, and plain unsweetened curd. Use measured oil. Adjust portions to the person’s needs and explain any assumptions.”
R — Response
Present the answer in this order:
1. Brief assumptions and safety note.
2. A 7-day table with meals, approximate portions, and simple preparation notes.
3. A grocery list grouped by vegetables, protein foods, grains, dairy, and pantry items.
4. Practical tips for meal prep, office hours, hydration, and handling hunger.
5. A short note on how to monitor progress and when to seek advice from a healthcare professional.
Use clear, supportive language. Avoid shame, rigid food rules, and claims that 10 kg in 3 months is guaranteed or appropriate for everyone.
